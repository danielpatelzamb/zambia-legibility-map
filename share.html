Zambia · mineral rights register · July 2026

The state keeps two registers of who mines. They do not agree

Every one of Zambia's 7,468 active mineral rights reconciled against the companies registry, the Mining Licensing Committee record, and sixty-two years of production. The interesting part isn't what either register says. It's where they contradict each other, how often the contradiction is the paperwork rather than the company, and where the record simply stops.

02, the disagreement

Which minerals sit under a non-mining declaration

A company's line of business comes from the companies registry. Its minerals come from the licence register. Tap a mineral to see how much of it is held by companies the registry does not classify as mining. This is a claim about the paperwork, not about the companies: see the caveats below.

03, a counting trap

Gemstones: twenty-eight licences, or two thousand?

Both. Only 28 licences were ever issued under a dedicated Gemstone Licence, a 2008-era category that has lapsed. Count the licence type and you miss the sector by seventyfold.

04: who can be found

The register gets darker as it gets smaller

Share of holders in each tier with any published way to reach them, a website, an email, a phone line, a business page. Artisanal rights are the largest cohort of people and the least contactable. Bidding areas are the tell: 1,442 licences held by four holders, because gazetted blocks sit with the state until allocated.

05, the money that moves

Offtakes and prepayments, not shipping manifests

Zambia is landlocked, so no ocean bill of lading is ever issued here and the shipper on a Durban manifest is a freight forwarder, not the mine. The value moves through prepayments, streams and corridor concessions instead.

06, the sector around the register

354 companies that never hold a mineral right

Traders, gemstone dealers, smelters, drilling and blasting contractors, equipment dealers, assay labs, hauliers, reagent suppliers. Most were found by sweeping the companies registry by keyword, which surfaces firms no directory lists.

What I got wrong on the way here

  1. 214, not 559. An earlier count of holders "absent from the companies registry" was mostly my own query bug: PACRA's search cannot handle ampersands, so VS Construction & Mining Services Limited returned nothing while VS Construction returned it, registered since 2008. Of 559 raw non-matches, 229 were cooperatives that register elsewhere and 116 were registered all along.
  2. A default notice is not a cancellation. The 18 June 2025 notice lists 3,402 licence codes, of which 137 are no longer on the active register. It is a show-cause notice of curable breaches: being listed is a prompt to verify, not proof of default.
  3. 121 companies are excluded from the declared-business percentages because the registry returned several candidate entities and the match was left unresolved rather than guessed.
  4. The registry's own classification is unreliable: this is the big one. It is a value captured on a registration form, not an audited account of activity. Rio Tinto Exploration Zambia is filed as "Construction of buildings"; Yetu Mining Limited as "Radio broadcasting". 96 of the 732 (13.1%) carry a mining word in their own registered name, and that is a measurable floor on the error rate, not a ceiling, it cannot catch a mis-filed holder whose name is not self-describing. Every figure here excludes the detectable cases, which is why it reads 27.7% and not 30.5%.
  5. Comtrade partners are financial, not physical. Counterparties include Swiss, Emirati and Singaporean trading desks that never see the metal.
  6. Contact details are deliberately not published here. They exist, held privately. For the 229 mining cooperatives none exists in any public source at all: those are reachable only through the national federation and provincial cooperative offices.
