Real licenses, real revocations — official government data

Provenance & how to read the flags: license parcels are the actual polygons served by the Geological Survey's open NGDR GeoServer (gsb.gov.zm WFS snapshot of the active register, 18 Jun 2025), shaded by license type — zoom in for individual parcel borders. Flags join the Ministry's MLC-78 cancellation list (Apr 2024) and the Final Public Default Notice (18 Jun 2025) by license code. Every parcel shown is on the active register, so none is currently cancelled. The 18 Jun 2025 "Default Notice" is a Section-72 show-cause notice listing curable breaches (unpaid area charges, unsubmitted quarterly/annual reports, unregistered pegging certificate) with a 30-day remedy window — being listed is a flag to verify, not proof of current default; a holder still on the register most likely cured it. MLC-78 flags are reinstated cancellations (adverse history). Amber = one such flag; red = both (repeat non-compliance). Absence from this snapshot proves nothing. Holder names are the government's own published records.

License & ownership datasets that actually exist

Revised verdict (2026-07-23): the Landfolio cadastre's ArcGIS endpoints are token-gated and third-party portals carry no Zambian license polygons — but the Geological Survey's NGDR GeoServer serves actual active-license polygons through an open WFS (~7,468 records, fresher than the portal itself), and the Ministry's cancellation/default notices publish license codes and holders. Joined by license code, those two official sources power the license and revocation layers on the map above. The remaining sources below cover mine points, footprints, contracts and company data.

    Who owns the ground — and who won't say

    Every organisational holder on the register (2,716) queried against PACRA's public companies-registry API, which returns statutory standing per company: whether beneficial ownership has been declared, whether annual returns are filed, and whether the company meets the minimum share capital. This is the layer the KYC tab used to have to send you to PACRA for.

    One number was corrected downward. An earlier pass counted 559 holders as absent from the companies registry. PACRA's search cannot handle ampersands — VS Construction & Mining Services Limited returns nothing while VS Construction returns it (registered 2008, active). Of the 559, 229 are cooperatives that register with the Registrar of Cooperatives instead, and 116 were registered all along. 214 are genuinely unexplained. 121 further companies are excluded from the declared-business figures because the registry returned several candidate entities and the match was left unresolved rather than guessed.

    Declared business vs mineral rights

    How the companies registry classifies each holder's line of business, set against what the licence register says it holds. 732 of 2,128 are not classified as mining — wholesale trade, building construction, road freight, mixed farming — covering 10.9 million hectares. Read that as a statement about the registry, not about the companies. The classification is a value captured on a registration form, not an audited account of activity, and it is provably wrong for some miners: Rio Tinto Exploration Zambia is filed as "Construction of buildings" and Yetu Mining Limited as "Radio broadcasting". Excluding every holder whose own registered name contradicts its classification, the figure is 27.7% of licensed ground — that is the defensible one.

A construction firm can legitimately hold a limestone right, and a farming company can own ground with copper under it — but the larger caveat is that the classification itself is unreliable. 96 of the 732 (13.1%) carry a mining word in their own registered name while being classified as something else.     Legibility by licence type — who can even be found

    The register is not uniformly opaque. Production and processing licences are held by companies you can mostly identify and sometimes reach; exploration is thinner; the artisanal tier is effectively dark. The Bidding area row is the giveaway — 1,442 licences held by 4 holders, because gazetted blocks sit with the state until they are allocated.

    Gemstones: 28 licences, or 2,056?

    Both, and the gap is the point. Only 28 licences were ever issued under a dedicated Gemstone Licence type — a 2008-era category that has effectively lapsed. But 2,056 licences actually list a gem commodity, held by 1,459 distinct holders. Gemstones in Zambia are mined under ordinary exploration, artisanal and small-scale licences, so counting the licence type understates the sector by roughly seventy-fold.

    The 189 processing licence holders

    Mineral Processing Licences — 195 licences, 189 distinct holders. These are the plants: smelters, refineries, leach and SX-EW circuits, gemstone cutting. 152 of the 195 appear in the 18 June 2025 default notice and 35 carry a 2024 cancellation flag. They are already plotted on the Legibility Map under the purple Processing filter.

    How to reach them

    The register names 3,622 distinct holders. Published contact channels exist for a small fraction, and for the 229 mining cooperatives, for none at all — four independent sweeps of the full licensing archive found no phone, email or address for any of them. The route in is institutional: 82 reachable offices below, from the national federation down to district councils.

    Why the cooperative column is zero. These societies register with the Registrar of Cooperatives — resolved to sit under the Ministry of Small and Medium Enterprise Development, not Commerce — so no companies-registry record exists. Their artisanal rights, roughly 2 to 33 hectares, fall below the environmental-filing threshold, so they never lodge the documents that would carry a proponent contact. 186 of 229 are located to district, which is what makes the provincial route usable.

    The rest of the sector: traders, processors, services and supply

    The licence register only shows who holds ground. These 354 companies are the sector around it — mineral traders and gemstone dealers, smelters and refiners, drilling and blasting contractors, equipment dealers, assay labs, hauliers and clearing agents, reagent and grinding-media suppliers. 299 are confirmed on an official register (PACRA number, NCC contractor grade or a ZRA clearing-agent licence); 78 have operations evidenced by a source beyond the registry. Contact details are held privately and are not in this app.

    How these were found, and what that means. Most came from sweeping PACRA's public registry API by keyword — "DRILLING", "GEMSTONE", "HAULAGE" and so on — which surfaces registered Zambian companies that no directory lists. That is why the majority are marked registry record only: the company provably exists and is classified, but no source beyond the register has been checked. Treat those as a verified universe, not a researched profile. The 78 marked operations evidenced have a company site, accreditation, tender award or news source behind them. PACRA's own business classifications are sometimes registration-form artefacts — one assay lab is filed under "hospital activities" — and those are flagged per record rather than repeated as fact.

    Unit-value spread: cathode vs. concentrate

    Distribution of monthly partner-level unit values (USD/tonne), Zambian exports 2023–2024. Cathode (7403.11) is grade-controlled — LME Grade A is a commodity — so its unit values should cluster tightly. Concentrate (2603) varies in grade, contract and documentation, so its spread is wide. Residual spread in the grade-controlled product is the suggestive signal for a documentation/legibility premium.

    Correlational, not causal. Unit-value spread reflects grade, firm size, contract type, destination and incoterms — not only paperwork. See Methodology for the causal DAG and the identification strategy this MVP cannot yet run.

    Verified counterparties — standard by standard

    "Verified" here means checkable against a named public record, not an endorsement. Four independent standards: beneficial ownership on the companies registry (PACRA), payment disclosure under EITI, physical flows on US customs manifests, and LME brand registration. Most of the sector clears none of them — that sparsity is the verification void.

    Supply-chain advisor — tell it what you're trying to do

    Describe the transaction and this builds a tailored brief: which corridor and port, which categories of counterparty you need and who is actually registered in them, which verification standards a lender or buyer will ask for, and — the part most advisors skip — which of those things do not exist in Zambia yet. Every recommendation is traceable to a dataset in this terminal, not to a generic playbook.

    Customs-manifest evidence — bills of lading

    US CBP sea-import manifests naming Zambian shippers: cargo, weight, load port and destination. This is what third-party verification looks like when it exists — a foreign customs service with no stake in the story recorded the container.

    The gap this table proves. These manifests exist only because the US publishes import manifests. Zambia's own export side — the ZRA CD-1 form chain — is not public, so the equivalent record for the ~75% of exports that go to China and the region cannot be checked by anyone outside government. Upstream of the port, nothing here verifies which licence the metal came off — that chain-of-custody layer does not exist yet in any public record.

    The causal problem with the "certification spread"

    The motivating claim — a trader's report of a ~2× price spread between no-paperwork and clean-paper material — is a single-interview datapoint and confounded. Any spread computed from Comtrade unit values is correlational. The DAG below shows the back-door paths.

    Clean identification would need a within-firm, within-grade before/after design: the same seller, the same product, priced before and after obtaining certification. Comtrade aggregates cannot support that design, so this MVP labels every spread it shows as suggestive.

    Mirror-trade method (GFI)

    Following Global Financial Integrity's trade-misinvoicing methodology: compare Zambia-reported exports against partner-reported imports for the same HS code and period. GFI flags flows where mirror volumes differ by >2.5% and the import/export unit-value ratio falls outside 0.8–1.8. Positive gaps (partners report more than Zambia) suggest export under-reporting. Known noise sources: CIF vs FOB valuation, re-export hubs (Switzerland, UAE, Hong Kong), and Zambia's landlocked transit through Tanzania, South Africa, Mozambique, Namibia and Angola.

    Honest limitations

    • Formal-economy bias (the central caveat): Comtrade, EITI, PACRA, the cadastre and the clearing-agent list all see only the formal economy. The informal/artisanal streams where the arbitrage is largest are invisible by construction — this map illuminates the formal shell and infers the void.
    • Cadastre: the Landfolio portal's ArcGIS query endpoint is login-gated; license markers here are illustrative, not cadastre records.
    • Preview-tier trade data: the free Comtrade preview caps 500 records/query; any capped query is flagged in the data file metadata.
    • Corridors are schematic — waypoint polylines, not surveyed alignments.
    • Legal transition: the Minerals Regulation Commission Act (No. 14 of 2024) may change portal URLs/access; re-verify sources before publication.
